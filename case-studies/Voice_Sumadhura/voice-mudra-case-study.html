01 / Project overview

From isolated call reviews
to a connected feedback loop.

Voice Sumadhura is a proposed AI-assisted platform for assessing customer conversations, documenting call-quality feedback, and supporting improvement across pre-sales teams.

01

Review the conversation

Bring recordings and AI-generated suggestions into a dedicated analyst workspace.

02

Share clear feedback

Notify an executive only after an analyst has finalized the review.

03

Understand progress

Give managers visibility into ratings, acknowledgements, disputes, and recurring issues.

Today, reviewers listen to recordings, document errors manually, and share feedback through disconnected channels. The proposed product brings analysis, human validation, employee acknowledgement, and manager visibility into one experience.

Project status: design stage. Benefits described here are goals, not measured outcomes.

02 / Defining the problem

The challenge wasn't just
listening to a recording.

Discussions with quality reviewers and pre-sales teams surfaced a wider operational problem: review effort, fragmented communication, and limited evidence of follow-through.

A

Slow manual review

Analysts listen through entire calls—reportedly around 25–40 minutes per reviewed recording—and document observations by hand.

B

Feedback is scattered

Written notes may be photographed and shared through messaging tools, making them harder to organize and revisit.

C

Follow-through is unclear

Reviewers cannot consistently tell whether an executive read a comment or avoided repeating the same mistake later.

How might we help analysts review customer conversations more efficiently, while making feedback clearer to executives and improvement easier to monitor?

05 / Proposed solution

Connect analysis, review,
and action.

Rather than letting AI publish a judgment automatically, the experience keeps the quality analyst responsible for validating what gets shared.

Import or sync calls→AI-assisted analysis→Analyst validation→Submit review→Executive acknowledgement→Manager visibility
01 · Analyst

A focused review workspace

Find a recording, listen to moments, compare AI suggestions with evidence, adjust the checklist, and submit a final review.

02 · Executive

Feedback at the right moment

Receive a notification only after submission, open the call's rating and notes, comment, acknowledge, or request re-review.

03 · Manager

A clearer team overview

Filter by executive, project, or date to understand trends, review changes, and follow-up status.

08 / Feedback and follow-through

From a submitted review
to a visible next step.

After an analyst submits a final review, the pre-sales executive receives a notification showing that particular call's rating—not their average across all calls.

Opening the notification reveals detailed findings, an explanation, and a required comment. The executive can acknowledge reading it or request a re-review. Disputed items return to the same analyst.

The executive can view only their own call records and feedback. A manager can see the team-wide review history.

09 / Key product decisions

Designing for trust
and accountability.

In an AI-assisted workflow, speed alone isn't enough. The system must make it clear who checks an output, who receives feedback, and how changes are handled.

Design principle: AI supports the review; it does not replace reviewer judgment.

01

Analyst-controlled approval

AI suggestions and ratings can be corrected, edited, or marked “Not a mistake.”

02

Clear notification timing

Pre-sales is notified on final review submission—not when a recording is uploaded.

03

Traceable changes

The proposed manager view exposes the original AI score, final score, changes, and acknowledgement history.

04

Protected project knowledge

Project data may be retrieved from approved sources, but only administrators edit the knowledge base; reviewers verify questionable claims.

05

Useful reporting

Dashboard summaries and daily, weekly, and monthly report emails, with an option to disable email notifications.

10 / Stakeholder alignment

Feedback shaped
the proposed flow.

I discussed the experience with quality checkers, pre-sales teams, project managers, and the pre-sales head. They responded positively to the simpler review and notification workflow.

Less manual follow-up

Stakeholders valued the possibility of feedback being automatically shared after submission.

Clearer mistakes

They wanted employees to understand what was wrong without searching through scattered paper notes.

Greater visibility

They saw value in knowing whether feedback had been acknowledged and checking subsequent call performance.

These are qualitative reactions to a design proposal. They are not measured usability-test results, production outcomes, or validated efficiency gains.

11 / Intended value & reflection

What success would look like.

Voice Sumadhura is still in design. The next step is to evaluate whether this approach makes review work faster and feedback easier to act on.

Quicker quality reviews

Track review time and calls reviewed per analyst before and after implementation.

Clearer follow-through

Track acknowledgement rates, review disputes, and the time between review submission and employee response.

Better coaching signals

Explore recurring mistake patterns and rating trends across repeated call reviews.

My biggest learning: workflow design is not just about automating tasks; it's about connecting people, decisions, and the information that helps them act.

No production adoption, time-savings percentages, satisfaction improvements, or booking gains are claimed. These would need testing and real data after implementation.
