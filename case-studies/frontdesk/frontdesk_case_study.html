01 / The context

One entry point.
Multiple operational needs.

Frontdesk was created to bring visitor registration, workplace entry tracking, courier handling, and management reporting into a structured operational experience.

As the end-to-end designer, I worked from requirement gathering and workflow planning through UI design, engineering collaboration, implementation support, and receptionist training.

01

For receptionists

Register different visit types, locate employee information, manage check-ins and status changes, and retrieve visit records.

02

For visitors

Provide essential information through assisted registration or a QR-led mobile flow, then receive an access pass.

03

For management

Review visitor activity and operational trends across locations, departments, and reporting periods.

02 / Defining the challenge

Designing for more than
the moment of arrival.

The challenge was not simply to collect a visitor’s details. The experience needed to work across different visitor categories, office teams, connected systems, and management needs.

A

Different entry scenarios

A customer, courier, interview candidate, channel partner, and VIP should not all be forced into an identical workflow.

B

Reliable information

Registration depended partly on Salesforce and Great HR data, where available fields did not always match the initial designs.

C

Visibility after check-in

Receptionists needed actionable records; management needed summarized, filterable, and shareable views across sites.

How might we make workplace check-in easier to operate while keeping visitor information, approval activity, and reporting organized across locations?

08 / Working through constraints

The design had to work
with real systems.

Technical feasibility became a key design consideration during handoff. Engineering raised implementation and timing constraints, while integration stakeholders clarified which information was available through Salesforce and Great HR.

01 · Feasibility

Revisited parts of the interface when proposed behavior could not be delivered within the available implementation window.

02 · Salesforce

Adapted fields and flows to the available Salesforce data, including gaps in project-related information.

03 · Great HR

Worked with HR and technical stakeholders to revise employee-related fields around API data availability.

What changed in my approach: the first design was a hypothesis, not a fixed specification. I adjusted scope and data capture so the experience remained implementable.

10 / Outcome and reflection

What the product delivered.

Connected entry workflows

A designed application experience for visitor, courier, employee and VIP-related check-in scenarios.

More structured operations

Visitor passes, status actions, searchable entry records and activity history in a shared system.

Management visibility

Location-aware dashboards, filterable reports, exports and planned scheduled email updates.

The most valuable lesson: successful enterprise design balances user workflows, business needs, system constraints and real-world adoption.

This case study intentionally does not claim measured time savings, user satisfaction gains or percentage improvement. Those metrics were not validated.
